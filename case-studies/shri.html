SHRI: Running 20 public toilets on data the whole team can see

SHRI runs free public toilets in Bihar and Jharkhand. Working with the Dalgo team, it turned the forms its facility staff fill in every day into reports and a public dashboard that update on their own. Problems get fixed in time, and funders, government and communities see the same numbers.

Partner
Sanitation and Health Rights in India (SHRI), Bihar and Jharkhand
Work
Free shared public toilets with daily operations and monitoring; 20 facilities, used over 2.2 million times in 2024
Tools
Kobo, Looker Studio
Engagement
Platform plus consulting. One of Dalgo's earliest partners; the reporting system was built with the Dalgo team

Key results

  • Problems fixed in timeOperations sees each facility's use, issues and staff check-ins every day and decides what to fix first
  • Every dayPublic dashboard refresh for funders, government and community, in Hindi and English
  • 99% uptimeDays facilities are open and working, now tracked daily
  • 5–7 hrs a week → automatedReport preparation, which once needed one person's know-how

The challenge

SHRI builds and runs public toilets for families who cannot build their own, then operates and monitors them so they stay clean and usable. Its goal is consistent toilet use over time. With 20 facilities and thousands of users a day, keeping every stall working becomes a data problem.

  • Reports that depended on one person. Building the weekly report took 5 to 7 hours and needed specialist knowledge.
  • No view of the ground. The team could not see in real time what was happening at each facility.
  • A dashboard built on manual entries. It showed whatever staff had entered, when they entered it.

How we worked together

SHRI built its reporting system with the Dalgo team as one of Dalgo's earliest partners. Facility staff kept the four Kobo forms they already used; the system connects them, so every submission flows into reports and dashboards without anyone rebuilding them.

  • Daily use. People-counting machines on the men's and women's sides, logged by staff each evening, with unusually high or low days easy to spot.
  • Staff check-in. A barcode scan on each staff ID card marks attendance at every facility.
  • Issue reporting. Twice a day, once per shift, staff log electrical, plumbing, infrastructure, supply, cleanliness and technology issues.
  • Weekly photos. Timestamped, geotagged photos of every facility, filterable by facility and date.

The transformation

Every Kobo form now feeds a daily report and a public dashboard, so each team acts on the same picture of all 20 facilities.

Operations and leadership. There was no real-time picture of the facilities. Now a daily report on use, attendance, issues and photos tells them which problem to fix first, how each facility is being used, and where quality is slipping.

Supervisors. They check whether staff checked in and cleaned on schedule at every facility.

Facility staff. They read the daily use report themselves.

Funders, government and community. The public dashboard once showed whatever staff had entered. It now updates every day, in Hindi and English, with maintenance logs, outages and staff attendance.

The weekly report that took 5 to 7 hours by hand is now generated automatically, and the system calculates total use, average daily use and cost per use.

What’s next

SHRI is starting monthly random spot checks with district water and sanitation committees to validate its data, and is working with the Government of Jharkhand on taking the model to more community toilets, schools and health facilities. With other NGOs, it calls this “Shri in a box”.
