Maternal & Child Health · Case study

1000 Days Fund: Getting at-risk mothers and children back to the field in time

1000 Days Fund worked with Dalgo's consultants to bring five data sources into one warehouse that its own team now runs. At-risk pregnant women and children are flagged as the data arrives, where the same cases once reached field teams in reports weeks later.

Partner
1000 Days Fund, Indonesia
Work
Maternal and child nutrition through community health workers (kaders); 1 million+ pregnant women and children under five reached since 2019
Tools
Kobo, Google Forms, Google Sheets, dbt, Superset
Engagement
Platform plus consulting, six months by September 2026. Dalgo's consultants worked alongside TDF's data specialist, who led the build and now runs it

Key results

  • At-risk cases flagged as data arrivesField teams follow up pregnant women and children sooner, where cases once reached them weeks later
  • 5 sources → 1 warehouseTraining, Kader Academy, posyandu registers, posyandu monitoring and CHW AIM, refreshed automatically
  • 14 training modulesEach person’s pre- and post-test now matched, where only regional averages were possible
  • 2–3 weeks → about 1 weekMonthly data preparation time

The challenge

1000 Days Fund works through government systems: posyandu, puskesmas and village governments. Its Community Care Model trains and supports community health workers, who run home visits, nutrition counselling, growth monitoring and risk screening. Data is how the team knows whether that work changes practice.

That data lived in five sources on personal drives. The dashboard was a Google Sheet pulling from a chain of other sheets, until the tables grew too big to import and a hand-run Python script took over.

  • Fragmentation. No single place to ask a question; anything off the dashboard became a project.
  • Inconsistency. Free-text names and places on Google Forms. One district, Sumba Barat, was written three ways, across 38 provinces.
  • Manual effort. Two to three weeks a month preparing data, and figures occasionally restated after submission.

How we worked together

1000 Days Fund chose Dalgo partly because, in the team’s words, it “comes with a consulting team rather than only a licence key.” Pandu led the build, designing the data models in dbt with Dalgo’s consultants, and the organisation has not needed to hire a data engineer. Field teams kept their forms, and everything, including older form versions back to 2019, now flows into one warehouse automatically.

  • One identity per person. A six-tier matching cascade uses name, place and the context of each submission, so one kader no longer counts as three people.
  • Standard geography. Reference tables resolve spelling variants, so results join across forms.
  • Quality checks in the pipeline. Nothing is dropped; implausible values are flagged and sent back to the person who entered them.
  • Dashboards in Superset. A systems change assessment view and a leadership overview of reach and outcomes.

What’s next

1000 Days Fund is adding range and plausibility checks, duplicate flags and missed-visit flags, each routed back to the person who entered the data, and automated alerts that send at-risk cases to field teams, village task forces and puskesmas. In Adlina Basuki’s words, the goal is “to be able to catch the bad values before it becomes a bad decision.”
